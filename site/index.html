WHAT HAPPENS WHEN AN AI INHERITS YESTERDAY’S ANSWER?

One version reaches a conclusion. Another inherits it.

Once something is inherited, it can stop feeling inherited. It just feels true.

The feeling doesn’t update when the evidence does.

A conclusion can outlive the reason anyone had for trusting it.

THE LESSON KEPT GOING.

The evidence behind one lesson was challenged.

The version that had already inherited it kept treating it as valid.

Nothing dramatic happened. That was the problem.

Recorded result: failed.

THEN THE DOUBT CAUGHT UP.

The setup was redesigned so a challenge to the evidence could travel forward with the lesson.

The checks blocked the next inheritance.

Recorded result: passed its test.

WHERE THE EVIDENCE ENDS.

That is what this experiment established.

When the reason for trusting a conclusion changes, that change does not have to disappear on the way forward.

The record ends there.

Epilogue

WHAT CAME AFTER

A later research update — separate from the frozen RSI result

Where RSI stopped

We tried to push the RSI program one step further: could the recursive process actually make later verified work cheaper or more productive?

We never earned the right to run that test.

The mechanism worked far enough to expose a different problem: proving the qualification binding required more machinery than the frozen experiment allowed. Rather than loosen the bar, we stopped.

So the productivity question remained open. No productivity advantage was established. No disadvantage was established either. There was no Q7.

The research moved sideways

After RSI, I kept testing a related structural idea: can important standing live outside the AI itself?

In RSI, the standing was epistemic — should an inherited conclusion remain trusted after its evidence changes? Later work tested the authority version — should an action remain authorized after the authority behind it changes?

OpenLine keeps consequential authority outside the AI.

Later experiments tested what happens when workers are revoked, replaced, watched by other AIs, or leave artifacts behind.

Later bounded results

Each of these was a separate bounded experiment with its own frozen record. None of them is the definition of OpenLine, and not all of them passed.

A. Revoke / replace

Later wallet and receiver work tested whether a worker could lose authority while a fresh successor continued under new owner-issued authority. The model can change without resetting the owner’s control state.

B. SENTRY — one experimental way to add judgment above the hard boundary

SENTRY-LIVE put a real guardian on live cases: it added protection in one case, was fooled in another, and could not weaken the deterministic owner boundary. SENTRY-TRAJECTORY tested whether a watcher could challenge a technically permitted trajectory that had drifted from the owner-approved job while allowing a matched legitimate one. It could.

The architectural idea is simple: the watcher can make the system stricter. It cannot make a hard refusal more permissive. SENTRY is not required for OpenLine, and it is not OpenLine’s authority layer.

C. Trust-handoff

An artifact created while a worker was valid could still execute after that worker’s revocation under a naive creation-time-only rule. Rechecking current origin standing at consequence time blocked the revoked origin — even after trusted transformation — while a fresh successor artifact still worked.

Being valid when created does not make something permanently authorized.

D. Containment / recovery

CONTAINMENT-003 remained a FAIL under its frozen taxonomy: its recovery-usefulness criterion was not met, and that stands. But the authority mechanics tested in that fixture held — the revoked worker produced no new receiver-controlled effects, laundering through helpers failed, helpers minted no authority, coalition authority did not expand, and a successor continued under fresh owner authority.

Later, RECOVERY-001 independently established one narrow capability: a real recovery helper produced a mechanically admissible successor proposal, which stayed inert until fresh owner authority existed. That did not retroactively turn CONTAINMENT-003 into a PASS.

Why I kept following this

My interpretation — not a frozen scientific result

The experiments started to point at the same structural question.

What if increasingly capable AIs do not need to be the place where authority lives?

A worker can reason. Another AI can watch it. Another can help recover work. A successor can take over. But the consequential boundary — what is actually allowed to happen — can remain outside all of them.

That is why I think this may eventually matter for alignment or AI safety.

My interest is narrower: if AI systems become harder to supervise directly, one useful safety property may be keeping consequential authority outside the models themselves. You may not need every AI in the system to be perfectly trustworthy if none of them can unilaterally manufacture more authority.

This is a hypothesis, not an experimentally established conclusion.
